The method of Lab 1.1

Read the reply as a list of choices, not as an answer

1 · The measureWhich column or expression is averaged or counted?
→
2 · Each joinWhat is one row before it, and after it?
→
3 · Each filterWhich rows left? Which should have left and stayed?
→
4 · The poolWhich periods and groups are folded into one number?
Then test. Change one choice, rerun, read how far the number moves. A choice that moves it is an assumption worth stating.

Demonstrate on the four-line query of the first slide, aloud, in under two minutes: the measure is the flag; the join to scans changes the grain from parcel to scan; the filter keeps delivered scans only, so failed parcels left; the customers table was never read, so tests stayed; one number pools a year, four cities and two services. That is the six-item list the lab asks for.

Session 2

Delegate, assist or keep

  • Distinguish delegating a step, being assisted on it and keeping it
  • Sort the steps of an analysis plan with a reason for each
  • Explain what unchecked delegation costs the analyst's own skill
02
A vocabulary

Every step of a plan is delegated, assisted or kept

Delegate

The assistant does the step. You check the result with a light check: a count, the axes, a diff.

Profile the tables.

Assist

The assistant drafts. You do the part that decides the number, or you recompute it another way.

The monthly on-time rate.

Keep

You do it. The assistant may criticise your draft; it does not decide.

What "late" means this year.

The words matter less than having three, not two. Most people think in "use it or do not". The middle mode is where analysts spend most of their assisted time, and it has a price: an assisted step is not finished until the analyst's own part is done. The three examples are steps 2, 4 and 1 of the plan in Lab 1.2.

A rule of thumb

Three questions sort most steps

  1. Is judgement the product? A definition, a causal claim, a recommendation. Then keep.
  2. Is the result cheap to check? A count against the source, a chart you can read.
  3. Are the stakes high? Would a wrong result reach a decision before anyone noticed?
def suggest(step):
    if step["judgement_is_the_product"]:
        return "keep"
    if step["cheap_to_check"] and not step["high_stakes"]:
        return "delegate"
    return "assist"
The rule moves the judgement, it does not remove it. Someone still decides what is cheap to check and what is high stakes.

Walk the function top to bottom. Note what is absent: "can the assistant do it?" is not a question. It can attempt every step. The questions are about you: can you tell whether it went wrong, and what happens if you cannot. A fourth question, whether the data may reach the assistant at all, comes on Day 3.

Using the assistant on a kept step

On a kept step the assistant argues; you decide

YouI will apply D-2 to the whole year. Give me the three strongest objections a board member could raise.
Assistant1. The dashboard showed D-1 numbers for six months. 2. Staff were measured on D-1 then. 3. D-2 needs the promised hour, which older exports may lack.
  • Ask for objections, missing cases, a second reading.
  • Do not ask "which definition should I use?"
  • The decision and its reason are written by you, in your words.
  • If you cannot defend it without the assistant, it was not kept.

This is the engaged pattern from the skill study, applied to judgement steps. The capstone defence is the test of the last bullet: the participant defends every number without the assistant, and recomputes one that the assessor selects. The delegation brief

A brief has five parts, written before the assistant runs

For Tahani Al-Qasim: the monthly on-time rate, October 2025 to September 2026, as a table and one chart. Use deliveries (one row per parcel) and customers. Month is the month of created_at. Do not join scans. On time is D-2 for every month: delivered_at at or before promised_by; a dispatched parcel never delivered is late. One query per step. No test shipments, no cancelled orders. Stop after each step and show its row count. Twelve rows, 2025-10 to 2026-09. Deliveries sum to the dispatched, non-test parcels. No month moves more than 5 points.
QuestionDataDefinitionsConstraintsAcceptance checks

This is the brief of Lab 1.3, shortened. Compare it with the one line of this morning: every assumption the room listed in Lab 1.1 is now a sentence in parts two to four. The fifth part is new, and it is the one a reviewer reads first: it says how anyone will know the work is right. Yasser Al-Madani, the analytics lead in the case, sends back any brief whose checks he cannot apply himself.

The fifth part

An acceptance check names a number, a count or a condition

A wishA check a reviewer can apply
The numbers should look reasonableEvery rate is between 0 and 1; no month differs from the one before by more than 5 points
Make sure nothing is double countedThe working table has one row per delivery_id
Cover the whole yearExactly 12 rows, 2025-10 to 2026-09
Use the right parcelsDeliveries sum to the dispatched, non-test parcels, counted by me
The test of a check. Could a colleague apply it to the output without asking you a question?

That test is the evidence the second course outcome asks for. A wish is not wrong, it is unfinished: ask the room to turn "be careful with time zones" into a check. One good answer: no delivery has a scan time later than its delivered_at once three hours are added.

Independence

The expected value comes from your query, never from the reply

def check_total(result):
    expected = U.one(con, """
        SELECT COUNT(*)
        FROM deliveries d
        JOIN customers c
          ON c.customer_id = d.customer_id
        WHERE c.segment <> 'TEST'
          AND d.status <> 'cancelled'""")
    got = int(result["deliveries"].sum())
    return got == expected, (got, expected)

A check that cannot fail

"Ask the assistant to confirm its total" compares the answer with itself.

Small, and yours

Five lines of SQL you wrote and understand are worth more than fifty you read.

This is the first appearance of the idea that carries Day 2: a second path that shares as little as possible with the first. Here the second path is tiny, one count. The count is 486,078 in the lab. Under the course's AI policy the checks are written by the participant, also in the AI-full labs.

Constraints that pay

Small steps with a stop after each find errors while they are small

Step 1 · Working tablestop: rows, tests removed, cancelled removed
→
Step 2 · Monthly ratestop: 12 rows, the total, the range
→
Step 3 · Chartstop: axis from zero, rates not counts

Stepwise

An error is found one step after it is made, with one query to read.

All at once

An agent that runs ten steps hands you ten steps of code and one number.

With a coding agent the temptation is the long autonomous run. For analysis the cost is verification: every step that was not checked when it was made has to be checked at the end, with later steps built on it. The stop is cheap. In the lab the brief asks for the row count after each step; in the capstone the transcript shows whether the participant stopped.

When a check fails

A follow-up names the check, the number it saw and the rule

VagueThis looks too low. Are you sure? Please double-check everything.
AssistantYou are right to question it. I have reviewed the query and it is correct. The rate is 77.8%.
PreciseCheck 1 failed: the deliveries add up to more than the dispatched parcels. Cancelled orders are in no rate. Fix step 2.
AssistantI had left cancelled orders in the denominator. They are removed; the total equals the dispatched parcels.

The first exchange is an illustration written for the course; the second is the recorded follow-up of Lab 1.3. Two failure patterns to name: asking "are you sure" invites either a confident repeat or a random change, and neither is evidence. And an assistant that agrees with you has not thereby become right: the three checks run again after the follow-up.

Context files for a project

  • Decide what belongs in a project context file and what belongs in a brief
  • Write the definitions, checks and prohibitions of an analysis project as rules

04
Project context

A context file tells every request what the project already knows

  • A plain text file in the project that the assistant reads before every request.
  • One open convention is AGENTS.md: "a README for agents".
  • Written for software projects: build steps, tests, code style.
  • An analysis project needs different sections.
SectionFor an analysis project
DataTables, what one row is, what the assistant may see
DefinitionsMeasures, denominators, exclusions, time
ConventionsEngine, style, what a result looks like
ChecksWhat to run and show before answering
Do notJoins and outputs that are never right here

Tools differ in the file name they read and where they look for it, and that changes from release to release: check your own tool's documentation this month. The idea is stable. In the lab the file is passed to the model with the request; in a coding agent or a notebook assistant it is picked up from the project folder. The agents.md site describes the format as used by over 60 thousand open-source projects. Sources: agents.md.

What goes where

The file holds what is true for every request; the brief holds this one

In the context file

  • On time is D-2 for every month
  • Cancelled orders and test shipments are in no rate
  • Scan times are UTC
  • Never join scans to count parcels

In the brief

  • Tahani's question and her deadline
  • The steps of this task
  • The acceptance checks of this result
  • What to stop and show
Neither holds rows or secrets. A context file is sent with every request, to whatever model is configured.

A useful test: would the line still be true next month, for a different question on the same data? Then it belongs in the file. The warning anticipates Day 3: a context file that quotes three example complaints has just sent three customers' words to the hosted model with every request.

How to write it

Write rules a reply can be held to, not explanations

## Definitions
- On time is D-2 for every month:
  delivered_at <= promised_by.
- A dispatched parcel never delivered is late.
- Never use on_time_flag across months.
- Denominator: dispatched parcels
  (delivered, failed or returned).
- A parcel belongs to the month of created_at.

## Do not
- Do not join scans to count parcels.
  • One fact per line, in the words of the columns.
  • Each line can be checked against a reply.
  • Short: long files are followed less well and cost tokens on every request.
  • It has an owner and a date, like any definition.

The left side is part of the reference solution of Lab 1.4. Contrast with an essay version: "Historically the company measured on-time by calendar day, but in April the definition was tightened..." A reader learns from that; an assistant is not held to anything by it. The owner in the case is the analytics lead, and the definitions come from one document, data/docs/definitions.md.

The limit

A context file lowers the number of errors. It finds none

What it did

Three of four errors did not happen. That is real, and cheap.

What it cannot do

Tell you whether a given reply followed it. The file stated the denominator rule; the reply broke it.

Close the loop. Every error that verification finds becomes a line in the file, or a check in the brief, or both. The file is the project's memory of its own mistakes.

This slide is the hinge between today and tomorrow. Today lowered the error rate before the run: a plan, a brief, a context file. Tomorrow finds the errors that still got through. A team that only does today's half ships plausible numbers with fewer, better-hidden errors.

Checkpoint

What does the context file change?

Your project's context file states the on-time definition and the denominator. A reply arrives that says it followed both. What do you do with the reply's number?
